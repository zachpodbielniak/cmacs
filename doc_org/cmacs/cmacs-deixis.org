#+TITLE: cmacs-deixis — "move THAT over THERE"
#+AUTHOR: Zach Podbielniak
#+OPTIONS: toc:t num:t

* Overview

Deictic voice: you say "move that over there", "summarise this", "close that" while pointing -- with the mouse, a VR laser or a tracked hand -- and each demonstrative is bound to what was being pointed at *at the moment the word was spoken*.  Not at the moment the transcript arrives: by then, a second or two later, the pointer has moved on to the second thing, which is exactly why "move that there" cannot be done by asking where the pointer is now.

It is a fusion layer over three things that already existed, and it adds no engine of its own:

| Piece                 | Where                                                             | What it gives                                                    |
|-----------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Words with times      | the recognizers ([[file:cmacs-sherpa.org][cmacs-sherpa]], whisper), =cmacs-deixis-result-words= | each word's offset inside the utterance's audio                  |
| A clock               | [[file:cmacs-voice-loop.org][cmacs-voice-loop]], =cmacs-voice-loop-segment-start-functions=      | when that audio began, on the pointer history's clock            |
| Where the pointer was | =cmacs/gowl/cmacs-gowl-pointer-history.c=, or a registered pointer | what was under the pointer at a past time                        |

Turn it on with =M-x cmacs-deixis-mode= (a global minor mode) alongside =cmacs-voice-loop-mode=.  There is no configure flag: the Lisp is always there, and the compositor's ring is part of =--with-cmacs-gowl=.

* What happens to an utterance

With the mode on, the voice loop hands every final utterance to =cmacs-deixis-filter= before its route (=cmacs-voice-loop-utterance-filter-function=).  The filter:

1. takes the recognizer's words and their times (=cmacs-deixis-result-words=);
2. finds the demonstratives (=cmacs-deixis-find=);
3. binds each to what the pointer rested on while it was spoken (=cmacs-deixis-resolve=);
4. runs a local command if the utterance is one (=cmacs-deixis-run-command=) -- no model involved, and nothing is sent anywhere;
5. otherwise sends the utterance on to the loop's route *annotated* (=cmacs-deixis-annotate=).

An utterance with no demonstrative passes through untouched.  A pending yes/no confirmation is answered before the filter is asked.

** The annotated form

#+begin_example
move that [that=①] over there [there=②]

[Pointing: what each marked word referred to when it was spoken]
① window: librewolf — 'wlroots docs' (client 12)
② monitor 1 (DP-2) at 2900,400
#+end_example

The legend names things by id -- client id, buffer name and position, monitor index -- so a tool-capable model can act on "that" with the tools it already has.  In keeping with [[file:cmacs-ai.org][cmacs-ai-view]] it is a pointer and never the thing: window titles and buffer names, never buffer text.  A demonstrative with nothing pointed at is marked =[that=?]= and the legend says so rather than inventing a referent.  A referent looked up against the screen as it is now (an Emacs buffer position, see below) is marked =[approximate]=.

On the =commands= voice route the utterance is left plain (the command router matches words); the bindings are in =cmacs-deixis-last-bindings=.  Set =cmacs-deixis-annotate-for-commands= to change that.

** Bindings and referents

=cmacs-deixis-resolve= returns one plist per demonstrative, in spoken order: =:word=, =:token=, =:index=, =:role=, =:time= (ms, or nil when unknown), =:approx= (the time is an estimate), =:referent= and =:marker= (1, 2, ...; words bound to the same thing share one).

A referent is a plist:

| Key           | Meaning                                                                                              |
|---------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =:kind=       | =window= (a desktop window), =buffer-position=, =region=, =point=, =file=, =url=, =monitor=, =tag=, =place= |
| =:label=      | a short human description, by id                                                                     |
| =:confidence= | 0..1                                                                                                 |
| =:device=     | which pointer answered                                                                               |
| by kind       | =:client= =:client-id= =:app-id= =:title=; =:buffer= =:window= =:position=; =:beg= =:end=; =:monitor= =:x= =:y=; =:path=; =:url=; =:tag= |
| =:stale=      | the window closed between the word and the lookup; there is no =:client=                             |
| =:approximate= | resolved against the screen as it is now, not as it was                                              |

** Rules

- *Explicit state beats pointing.*  With the region active, an =object= word ("this", "that") or "it" means the region -- unless the pointer clearly rested on a different window (confidence at least =cmacs-deixis-elsewhere-confidence=, default 0.8).  A =place= word is never the region.  The region is tested when the transcript arrives, not when the word was spoken.
- *The longest rest wins.*  People point a little before or after the word, so the pointer is asked about a window from =cmacs-deixis-window-before-ms= (150) before the word starts to =cmacs-deixis-window-after-ms= (300) after it ends, and the answer is where it dwelt longest there, not where it was at one instant.
- *"It"* is bound only when it can refer to something: to what was pointed at while it was said, else to the =object= word before it in the same utterance (same marker).  With neither, it is left alone.  "It is raining" is never a demonstrative (=cmacs-deixis-pronoun-blockers=).
- *"That" as a conjunction* ("I think that we should") is skipped by looking at the next word (=cmacs-deixis-clause-followers=).  This is a word list, not a parser; it will be wrong sometimes, and the cost of a wrong binding is an extra marker in the annotation.
- *Nothing pointed at*: "this" and "here" fall back to point in the selected window with confidence 0.3 (=cmacs-deixis-fallback-to-point=).  "It" never falls back.

The vocabulary is =cmacs-deixis-words=, an alist of word to role (=object=, =place=, =pronoun=); replace it for another language.

* Word times

=cmacs-deixis-result-words RESULT= returns =((WORD START-MS END-MS . FLAGS) ...)=, times counted from the start of the audio the recognizer was given.  In order of preference:

1. *The result's own =:words=.*  New: sherpa-onnx offline (=asr=) and streaming (=stream=) results carry an optional =:words= entry, a list of =(WORD START-MS END-MS)=, whenever the model gives token timestamps (transducers, CTC, paraformer).  It is built in C (=build_words= in =cmacs/sherpa/cmacs-sherpa-asr.c=) from the token array that used to be flattened into one segment: subword pieces are merged into words (a piece starting with SentencePiece's =▁= or a space starts a word; punctuation stays on the word it follows; a CJK character is a word by itself), a word ends where the next begins and no later than its last piece's start plus the piece's duration (300 ms when the model gives none).  Existing keys are unchanged, and a model without timestamps simply has no =:words=.  For a stream the times are on the stream's axis, the one =:start= and =:end= are on, and =cmacs-speech='s stream =on-final= alist now passes =:words= through.
2. *=:tokens= with =:timestamps=*, merged in Lisp by =cmacs-deixis-merge-tokens= (the same rules).
3. *=:segments=* (whisper): each segment's words are spread across the segment's span in proportion to their length, and flagged =:approx= (=cmacs-deixis-interpolate-words=).  No change was needed in =cmacs/whisper/=.
4. *Only =:text=*: =cmacs-deixis-fallback-word-ms= (350) per word, =:approx=.

The text the loop sends has the wake phrase cut off the front, so its tokens are matched to the recognizer's words from the end; a token spelt differently ("21" against "twenty one") takes a time interpolated from its neighbours.

* The clock, and how wrong it can be

A word's absolute time is

: segment start + word offset + cmacs-deixis-latency-ms

where the segment start is taken when the voice loop opens a speech segment: =cmacs-voice-loop-segment-start-functions= runs with the segment id and the audio the segment already holds (pre-roll plus the onset frames), and deixis records =now - lead=.  "Now" is =cmacs-deixis-now-ms=: =gowl-pointer-history-now= (CLOCK_MONOTONIC milliseconds, the clock the compositor's ring stamps samples with) when the compositor is built in, else wall time, which is then only ever compared with samples stamped by the same function.

*None of the following has been measured.*  It was not possible to run audio while this was written; the figures are reasoning about the code, and the first one must be calibrated on real hardware.

| Source of error                            | Expected size                                  | Direction                         | Handled by                         |
|--------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------+------------------------------------|
| Capture latency: microphone, PipeWire/GStreamer buffers, delivery to Emacs in chunks | tens of ms to ~100 ms; depends on the device  | words look later than they were   | =cmacs-deixis-latency-ms= (default -60, an assumption) |
| Frame quantisation of the segment start    | up to one 32 ms frame                          | either                            | the window                         |
| Pre-roll accounting                        | should be exact (frames are counted)           | --                                | =LEAD-MS=                          |
| Emacs busy when the frame arrives (GC, redisplay) | usually a few ms, occasionally 100+           | words look later                  | the window; not correctable        |
| Token timestamps (sherpa)                  | one encoder frame, 40-80 ms; a token's time is when it was emitted, and streaming models emit late | usually later                     | the window; latency                |
| Interpolated word times (whisper)          | hundreds of ms inside a long segment           | either                            | flagged =:approx=; use a token-timestamp model |
| Pointer sampling                           | 20 ms (gowl), 100 ms (Lisp-sampled pointers)   | --                                | the window                         |
| The speaker                                | people point 100-300 ms before or after the word | either                            | the window and the dwell           |

Consequences worth knowing: with a whisper model the binding is only as good as the interpolation, which is fine for a short "close that" and unreliable for two demonstratives close together in a long sentence; a sherpa token-timestamp model is the right engine for this.  The streaming recognizer's assumption that token times count from the segment start (so the segment's start offset is added) was read from sherpa-onnx's sources, not observed.

** Calibrating

=M-x cmacs-deixis-calibrate=: say "now" to the voice loop and press a key at the same instant.  When the transcript arrives it reports the difference between the key's time and the word's, and the =cmacs-deixis-latency-ms= that would make it zero.  Your reaction time is in that number, so do it a few times and take the middle.  With a whisper model the message says the word time was itself an estimate.

* Pointers

** The gowl pointer history

"Motion never reaches Lisp" is the rule here as elsewhere: the pointer is sampled in C.  =cmacs/gowl/cmacs-gowl-pointer-history.c= keeps a fixed ring of 512 samples -- monotonic ms, layout x and y, the id of the client under the pointer (0: none), the monitor index -- filled by a 20 ms timer on gowl's own =wl_event_loop= (never GLib's default context, which under cmacs is Emacs's thread) and written under the gowl lock.  The timer exists only while the history is enabled.  A tick stores a sample only when something changed, so an entry means "from this time on"; the ring covers about ten seconds of a pointer that never rests and far longer otherwise.  No allocation per sample.  Nothing is sampled while the session is locked.

| DEFUN                                               | Returns                                                                                                         |
|-----------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =(gowl-pointer-history-enable BOOL)=                | non-nil when sampling is now on; nil with no compositor.  Turning it off empties the ring.                      |
| =(gowl-pointer-history-enabled-p)=                  | whether it is sampling                                                                                          |
| =(gowl-pointer-history-at MS)=                      | =(:time :x :y :client :client-id :monitor)= for the latest sample at or before MS; nil outside the ring         |
| =(gowl-pointer-history-dwell FROM-MS TO-MS &optional RADIUS)= | the same plus =:duration=, for the run of samples (same window, within RADIUS px, default 48) that lasted longest in the window; x and y are its time-weighted mean |
| =(gowl-pointer-history-now)=                        | the monotonic clock in ms                                                                                       |

Clients are stored by id, never by pointer.  A lookup turns an id back into an object only after finding it in the live client list, so =:client= is nil for a window that has closed and =:client-id= still says which it was.  The resolver turns that into a =:stale= referent: no command acts on it, and the legend reads "a window that has since closed (client 12)".

Every DEFUN that touches the ring takes =cmacs_gowl_lock_scoped ()= and returns through =unbind_to=.  =gowl-stop= must call =cmacs_gowl_pointer_history_forget ()= from =cmacs_gowl_forget_compositor_state= (the timer is a source on the compositor's loop); as a second line of defence the file remembers which compositor owns its timer and never removes a source from another one's loop.

When the window under the pointer is Emacs's own frame, the referent is refined to the Emacs window and buffer position there with =posn-at-x-y=.  That lookup happens when the transcript arrives, against the windows as they are then, and assumes the frame's pixel coordinates are the layout position minus the client's origin (true for an undecorated frame at output scale 1; unverified at other scales).  Such referents are flagged =:approximate=.

** A bare hand

Without =cmacs-handtrack-pointer-mode= -- or for the hand that is not driving the pointer -- you point by holding the pointing pose in front of the camera: index finger out, the other three curled.  Say "that" while the fingertip rests over a window.

*What this is and is not.*  One camera sees where a fingertip is in its image; it cannot see where the finger is aimed.  So a bare hand is a cursor held in the air, not a ray: the index fingertip is mapped onto the screen through the same active region and output the hand cursor uses (=cmacs-handtrack-pointer-region=, =cmacs-handtrack-pointer-output=), by the same C function.  A fingertip at the region's edge is at the screen's edge; one more than a tenth of the region beyond it points at nothing.  With =cmacs-handtrack-pointer-output= nil the output is the one the mouse pointer is on, so a bare hand cannot reach a second monitor unless that option names it.  A finger aimed straight at the camera is foreshortened to a stub and does not read as pointing; hold it up, as you would to touch a pane of glass.  Every hand referent is flagged =:approximate= and its confidence is capped (=cmacs-deixis-hand-confidence=, 0.9).

*The pose* (=cmacs_ht_point_pose= in =cmacs/handtrack/cmacs-handtrack-point-history.c=, pure: 26 joints in, a verdict out).  For each finger the classifier takes its /reach/: how much further from the wrist the fingertip is than its own knuckle, in metres on the hand scaled to the standard palm (0.094 m), which is what makes the pinch thresholds distance-independent too.  A straight finger reaches about 0.075; one folded into the palm about zero or less.  Pointing is index reach of at least =cmacs-handtrack-point-extend= (0.055) with none of middle, ring and little past =cmacs-handtrack-point-curl= (0.035), and the thumb not on the index tip (that is a pinch, which means something else).  The thumb is otherwise ignored.  Confidence is 0.5 exactly on a threshold and 1.0 from 0.02 m clear of both.

*The ring.*  512 fixed entries: monotonic ms, hand, pose confidence, the position as fractions across the output and as layout pixels, the id of the client under it, the monitor index.  One entry per camera frame in which a hand was pointing and none otherwise -- unlike the mouse ring an entry is an event, not a state -- so about seventeen seconds of uninterrupted pointing.  It is appended on the main thread, where inference results arrive (=ht_on_result=), from C structs only: no allocation, no Lisp object, nothing that can signal.  The position is One-Euro smoothed (=cmacs-handtrack-point-min-cutoff=, =-beta=), restarting whenever the hand leaves the pose so it does not glide in from where it last was.  What is under the fingertip is the compositor's own hit test, asked through =cmacs_gowl_pointer_history_hit=, which takes the gowl lock itself; on a locked session it refuses and nothing is stored.  With no compositor only the fractions mean anything, and they are resolved against Emacs's frames when the word arrives.

*The clock* is the mouse ring's, CLOCK_MONOTONIC milliseconds.  A sample carries the time its camera frame /arrived/ (taken on the camera's streaming thread, before inference), not the time its result reached the main thread, so the tens of milliseconds the networks take are not in it.  What is left is the camera's own delay -- exposure, readout, transport -- which the pipeline does not report: =cmacs-handtrack-point-latency-ms= (40, an assumption) is taken off every sample, and =M-x cmacs-handtrack-point-calibrate= measures it (pinch with one hand, press a key with the other at the same instant; the frame the pinch closed in is stamped the way pointing samples are).  In a build with hand tracking and no compositor =cmacs-deixis-now-ms= is wall time; the provider measures the constant between the two clocks and applies it.

*A rest is required.*  =cmacs-handtrack-point-history-dwell= groups one hand's samples into rests -- over the same window, within =cmacs-handtrack-point-radius= (96 px) of where the run began, with no gap over 250 ms -- and answers with the one that lasted longest inside the word's window, but only if it lasted =cmacs-handtrack-point-dwell-ms= (120) there.  A hand passing through the pose on its way somewhere else refers to nothing, and the provider does not fall back to wherever the hand was at the word's instant.

| DEFUN                                                                    | Returns                                                                                                                              |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =(cmacs-handtrack-point-history-enable BOOL)=                            | non-nil when pointing is now being noted.  Turning it off empties the ring.                                                          |
| =(cmacs-handtrack-point-history-at MS)=                                  | =(:time :x :y :client-id :monitor :hand :confidence :u :v :layout)= for the latest sample at or before MS, nil if none within 250 ms |
| =(cmacs-handtrack-point-history-dwell FROM-MS TO-MS &optional RADIUS MIN-MS)= | the same, means over the rest, plus =:duration= and =:since= (when the rest really began, possibly long before FROM-MS)         |
| =(cmacs-handtrack-point-history-clear)=                                  | empties the ring                                                                                                                     |
| =(cmacs-handtrack-point-history-status)=                                 | =:enabled :samples :capacity :stored :pointing :pinch-left :pinch-right :latency-ms :now=                                            |
| =(cmacs-handtrack-point-history-now)=                                    | the monotonic clock in ms                                                                                                            |

The plists have the mouse ring's keys so the resolver treats them alike, with one difference: there is no =:client=, only =:client-id=, and the provider looks the id up among the live clients (=gowl-list-clients=).  From there a hand sample goes through =cmacs-deixis--gowl-referent= exactly as a mouse sample does -- a desktop window, refined to the Emacs window and buffer position inside Emacs's own frame, a =:stale= referent for a window that has closed, else the monitor.

The ring is on only between the provider's =:start= and =:stop= (while deixis is sampling), and it is emptied when it is turned off *and* whenever hand tracking closes: where you pointed does not outlive the session that saw it.

There is no on-screen marker of where a bare hand is taken to be pointing.  Nothing existing draws one cheaply -- the hand cursor is the real pointer, the VR laser lives in the 3D scene -- so the feedback is after the fact: the echo area names what each hand-pointed word resolved to before a command acts on it (=cmacs-deixis-announce-hand=).

** Which pointer is believed

Every registered pointer is asked, and =cmacs-deixis-choose-referent= picks among the answers.  The two kinds fail in opposite ways: a mouse always has an answer, because it is always somewhere; a hand has one only while it holds the pose.  "First answer wins" would let whichever is listed first shadow the other for good.  So a referent may say when its pointer came to rest on it (=:since=), and one that came to rest more than =cmacs-deixis-parked-ms= (2000) before the word's window opened is /parked/ -- where it was left, not where anyone is pointing.

- The first candidate, in =cmacs-deixis-pointer-order=, that is not parked wins.
- When every candidate is parked, the first.
- A pointer that gives no =:since= is taken at its word.

A mouse left lying where it was does not outvote a hand raised to point, whichever is listed first; a hand left lying in the pointing pose does not outvote a mouse just moved; when both are in use the order decides.  For the gowl pointer =:since= is the time of the ring entry in force when its dwell began (a mouse nobody is touching makes no new entries); for the Lisp-sampled pointers, the ring entry's own time; for a hand, the first sample of the rest.  The function is pure -- referents in, one of them out.

** Adding one

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(cmacs-deixis-register-pointer
 'gaze
 (lambda (ms)
   ;; `cmacs-deixis-lookup-window' is (FROM-MS . TO-MS) around the word.
   (when-let* ((hit (my-gaze-history-at ms)))
     (list :kind 'buffer-position :window (car hit) :buffer (window-buffer (car hit))
           :position (cdr hit) :confidence 0.7
           :label (format "buffer: %s" (buffer-name (window-buffer (car hit)))))))
 :start #'my-gaze-history-start
 :stop #'my-gaze-history-stop)
#+end_src

=:start= and =:stop= are called when sampling begins and ends.  A pointer must hold positions only between the two, and only in memory.  One that signals costs its own answer, not the utterance.

* Commands

Simple utterances run locally.  An utterance is reduced to a canonical form -- lower-cased, =cmacs-deixis-filler-words= dropped, each bound demonstrative replaced by =<object>= or =<place>= -- and matched against each command's regexp: "Please move that window over there" is =move <object> <place>=.  A command runs only when every placeholder has a referent that still exists; otherwise the utterance goes on to the route, annotated, so a model can ask what was meant.

| Command | Says                                         | Does                                                                                                                                         |
|---------+----------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =move=  | move / put / send / bring / take that there  | a desktop window: to the pointed tag; onto another window of the same screen (a swap, the only "there" a tiling layout has); else to the pointed monitor, and a floating window also takes the pointed half.  An Emacs buffer: into the pointed Emacs window |
| =close= | close / quit / delete / kill that            | closes the desktop window, or deletes the Emacs window ("kill" kills the buffer).  Confirmed first                                           |
| =focus= | focus / go / switch / select / jump there    | focuses the desktop window, or selects the Emacs window and goes to the position                                                             |
| =swap=  | swap / exchange this and that                | two desktop windows (=gowl-swap-clients=), or two Emacs windows (=window-swap-states=)                                                       |
| =open=  | open / show / visit that here                | the file or URL at the pointed buffer position, in the pointed Emacs window                                                                  |

They use existing DEFUNs only (=gowl-move-client-to-monitor=, =gowl-client-set-geometry=, =gowl-set-tags=, =gowl-swap-clients=, =gowl-close-client=, =gowl-focus-client=).  A window embedded in an Emacs window is not moved (its place belongs to Emacs).  =open= reads the one file name or URL at the position, locally, to act on it; that is the only place buffer text is read, and it is not sent anywhere.

Destructive commands ask first (=cmacs-deixis-confirm-destructive=): through =cmacs-voice-confirm= -- a spoken yes/no or y/n, with the loop armed so the answer needs no wake phrase -- when the voice commands are loaded, else =y-or-n-p=.  The question names what would be closed, whatever pointed at it, and ends "(pointed at by hand)" when a bare hand did.

A bare hand's aim is approximate, so any command acting on a hand referent first says in the echo area what each word was taken to mean -- "Deixis (by hand): that = window: librewolf -- 'wlroots docs' (client 12)" (=cmacs-deixis-announce-hand=).  Labels only, as in the legend.

** Defining one

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(cmacs-deixis-define-command pin
    "\\`\\(?:pin\\|keep\\) <object>\\'" (that)
  "\"Pin that\": make the pointed window sticky."
  (unless (plist-get that :client)
    (user-error "That is not a desktop window"))
  (gowl-set-client-sticky (plist-get that :client) t))
#+end_src

The argument list names the referents in spoken order.  The body may start with a docstring, then =:confirm FORM= (non-nil: confirm first; a string is the question).  It reports a refusal with =user-error=, and can tell its verbs apart through =cmacs-deixis-command-words= (the canonical form).  Commands run from the command loop (=cmacs-voice-run-later=), so they may select windows.

* Privacy

- The pointers are sampled only while =cmacs-deixis-mode= is on *and* the voice loop is listening (its state is neither =idle= nor =paused=).  The mode follows =cmacs-voice-loop-state-change-functions=.
- Samples live in fixed rings in memory: the compositor's 512 entries, the hand's 512, and at most 128 entries for each Lisp-sampled pointer.  Stopping empties them.
- The hand's ring is also emptied whenever hand tracking closes (=cmacs-handtrack-close=, so turning =cmacs-handtrack-mode= off or losing the camera), and holds nothing for the hand driving the pointer.  No camera frame and no landmark is kept: a sample is a time, a hand, a confidence, a position and a client id.
- Nothing is written to disk and nothing is sampled on a locked session.
- What reaches a model is the legend: ids, titles, buffer names and positions.  Never buffer text, never coordinates of anything but the pointed spot.

* Voice-loop additions

Three additive hooks in =lisp/cmacs/cmacs-voice-loop.el=, usable by anything:

- =cmacs-voice-loop-segment-start-functions= -- =(SEG LEAD-MS)= when a speech segment opens; =LEAD-MS= is the audio already in it.
- =cmacs-voice-loop-utterance-filter-function= -- =(TEXT SEG RESULT)= before the route; returns the text to send, or nil when it took the utterance.  A filter that signals does not eat the utterance.
- =cmacs-voice-loop--final-result= -- the recognizer result of the utterance being dispatched.
