#+TITLE: GNU's Eye — Live Planetary Situational-Awareness Globe
#+AUTHOR: Zach Podbielniak
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 num:t

* Overview

GNU's Eye (~gnuseye~) is a Google-Earth / Palantir-style live 3D globe
embedded in a cmacs buffer.  It overlays everything that can be tracked on
and above the Earth — satellites and spacecraft, aircraft, marine vessels,
weather and geo-events — as clickable, heading-oriented markers on a
textured globe.

The globe is *rendered through the libregnum subsystem*: a gnuseye buffer
owns a libregnum view whose render context holds a persistent textured
Earth sphere plus per-tick marker and arc drawables built from live feeds.

The design goal is *extensibility*: data sources are **layers defined in
Elisp** via ~cmacs-gnuseye-define-layer~, over a tiny, stable C boundary.
Adding a new feed is one macro call — no rebuild.

Enable with ~--with-cmacs-gnuseye~ (requires ~--with-cmacs-libregnum~ and
~--with-cmacs-glib~).  Part of the standard cmacs flag set: ~just
bootstrap~ and the container build (~Containerfile~) enable it by default.

* Quick start

#+begin_src elisp
(require 'cmacs-gnuseye)
(cmacs-gnuseye)            ; M-x cmacs-gnuseye — opens *GNU's Eye*
#+end_src

~M-x cmacs-gnuseye~ opens a *dashboard*: the globe viewport in the centre;
on the left an *entity-type toggle* pane over a searchable/filterable
*entity list*; and an *inspector* on the right.  (A prefix arg opens just
the globe.)

*Everything starts off.*  The top-left pane is a checkbox per entity type
(Aircraft, Satellites, Vessels, Quakes, Launches, Fires); ~SPC~/~RET~ (or a
click) toggles rendering of that type on/off (☑/☐).  Enabling a type starts
its data feed and draws it; disabling clears it.  Navigation works in both
vanilla Emacs (~n~/~p~, arrows) and Doom/Evil (~j~/~k~, motion state).

In the globe viewport: drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan,
*hover* a marker to see its label, and *click* it to select it (recentre +
inspector).  Keys: ~e~ entity list, ~i~ inspector, ~l~ layers, ~s~ search,
~F~ filter by kind, ~c~ clear filters, ~g~ refresh, ~f~ fly-to lat/lon, ~?~
legend, ~q~ quit.

The globe is a sun-lit 3D sphere (soft day/night terminator; the night
side stays partly lit so markers everywhere remain readable) drawn as a
real *political map* from Natural Earth + flagcdn data (downloaded +
cached once), all in the globe's own lat/lon convention so it always lines
up with the markers --- a plane over France is over France:

- coastlines (~cmacs-gnuseye-coastlines~)
- country borders (~cmacs-gnuseye-borders~)
- country name labels, shown once zoomed in (~cmacs-gnuseye-labels~)
- admin-1 states/provinces for ~cmacs-gnuseye-admin1-countries~ (default
  USA + Canada; ~cmacs-gnuseye-admin1~)
- country flags as camera-facing billboards, shown once zoomed in
  (~cmacs-gnuseye-flags~; first load trickles in, then cached)

Colours are configurable (~cmacs-gnuseye-coastline-color~,
~-border-color~, ~-admin1-color~, ~-label-color~).

(A raster ~cmacs-gnuseye-base-texture~ is also supported but experimental:
the sphere mesh's UV layout does not match the marker convention, so a
photographic texture will not align with the markers --- prefer the
coastlines for an accurate, aligned Earth.)

Markers are recognisable, heading-oriented 3D icons: aircraft are little
planes (tapered nose, wings, engines, tail) floating at
exaggerated-but-readable altitude with a drop-line to their ground point;
ships are hulls with a bow, superstructure and funnel; satellites are
winged bodies high above with an orbit trail; rockets stand on the pad;
earthquakes are magnitude-sized spheres.  Zoom in to resolve the shapes;
the inspector reports the true altitude.

** Dashboard: list, inspector, search, filter

The *entity list* (~e~) shows every live entity (kind, label, lat/lon,
altitude, layer).  ~RET~ (or click) selects a row: the inspector fills in,
and the globe recentres on it and highlights it (enlarged, brightened,
labelled).  Clicking a marker on the globe does the same and moves point to
its list row.

- ~s~ *search* narrows the list to rows whose label/id/kind match a string.
- ~F~ *filter by kind* (completing-read-multiple) restricts BOTH the list
  and the globe to chosen kinds --- e.g. only planes, only boats, or planes
  and boats --- so you can declutter to what matters.  ~c~ clears filters.

Navigation works in vanilla Emacs and in Evil/Doom: the globe viewport runs
in Evil ~emacs~ state (its single-key commands reach the keymap), and the
list/layers panels run in ~motion~ state so ~hjkl~ navigation works while
~RET~/~s~/~f~/~g~ act on the current row.

~M-x cmacs-gnuseye-layers~ opens the layer control panel (toggle layers
with ~RET~; ~g~ refreshes the list).

** Entity-type toggle pane (categories)

The top-left pane organises layers into *collapsible categories* (Air
traffic, Astronomical, Marine, Weather & events, Natural events, Space
weather, Conflict & geopolitics, Infrastructure, Health & society, Media &
cameras, Markets).  A row whose id is a category header shows ~▸~/~▾~ and a
count of how many of its layers are on; ~SPC~/~RET~ on a header expands or
collapses it, on a layer toggles that layer.  *Everything starts off and
every category starts collapsed*, so the globe opens clean (map only) and you
opt into exactly the feeds you want.  Order and titles are configurable via
~cmacs-gnuseye-group-order~ and ~cmacs-gnuseye-group-titles~.

* Globe visualization

Beyond markers and trails the globe supports:

- *Day/night terminator.*  The globe is lit by the real Sun: the subsolar
  point is computed for the current time and fed to the globe shader, so the
  lit hemisphere tracks the actual time of day (refreshed every
  ~cmacs-gnuseye-sun-interval~ seconds; toggle with ~cmacs-gnuseye-day-night~).
  The night side stays partly lit with a cool tint so markers remain readable.
- *Filled polygons.*  Translucent regions draped on the sphere — weather
  *alert zones* (US NWS), aurora bands, country *choropleth* fills, and any
  AOI.  An entity may carry a ~:polygon~ ring (drawn per-tick, cleared with
  the markers); persistent fills (choropleth/aurora) are added directly via
  ~cmacs-gnuseye-add-polygon~ with PERSISTENT non-nil.  Fills are triangulated
  as a polar grid of great-circle rings so they drape correctly at any size,
  alpha-blended and two-sided.
- *Range / coverage rings.*  ~cmacs-gnuseye-circle-points~ (built on
  ~cmacs-gnuseye-destination~) returns a small-circle of a given radius about
  a point — for satellite footprints, base/detection radii, and geofence
  circles — usable as a polyline outline or a filled footprint.
- *Great-circle routes.*  ~cmacs-gnuseye-great-circle~ samples a route
  (trade lanes, cables, flight paths) for use as a ~:trail~.
- *Choropleth.*  ~cmacs-gnuseye-choropleth~ fills countries by a scalar map
  (ISO-A3 → 0..1) through a green→red translucent ramp on the cached Natural
  Earth admin-0 set; ~cmacs-gnuseye-choropleth-clear~ removes it.  The
  intelligence layer's country-instability index drives it.
- *Low-zoom clustering.*  Dense point layers declared ~:cluster~ (aircraft,
  vessels, GDELT) collapse nearby markers into a single count badge when
  zoomed out, so a continent's worth of aircraft reads as a few numbers
  instead of an unreadable pile.  Cluster cells are stable, so they don't
  flicker; clicking a badge flies in and dissolves it.  Toggle with
  ~cmacs-gnuseye-cluster~.

* Layer lifecycle and motion

** Atomic enable / disable

Toggling a type off is *atomic and complete*: its refresh timer is
cancelled, its entities are dropped from the index, and all its markers,
arcs, and per-tick polygons are cleared from the globe immediately.  A
*generation token* on each layer is bumped on every enable and disable, and
every async fetch captures the token at dispatch; a response that arrives
after the layer was toggled (a slow HTTP reply still in flight) finds a stale
token and is dropped silently.  So a disabled layer can never be re-drawn by
an in-flight update — no ghost markers, no flicker back to life.

** Dead reckoning (smooth motion)

Every layer whose entities move declares an ~:advance~ hook.  Between the
(slower) data fetches the shared smooth-motion tick
(~cmacs-gnuseye-smooth~, every ~cmacs-gnuseye-smooth-interval~ ≈ 0.4 s) calls
it to glide each entity along its current heading and speed; the next fetch
*course-corrects* the extrapolated position back to truth.  Aircraft and
vessels great-circle dead-reckon (the shared
~cmacs-gnuseye-dead-reckon-layer~); satellites re-propagate their orbit.  Any
new moving layer gets the same behaviour by setting
~:advance #'cmacs-gnuseye-dead-reckon-layer~ and reporting ~:speed~ (m/s) +
~:heading~.

* Interaction and intelligence

These are loaded from ~cmacs-gnuseye-feature-files~ when the globe opens.

- *Search everything + command palette.*  The entity-list search (~s~)
  matches across all fields including every ~:data~ value (callsign, MMSI,
  NORAD id, country).  ~cmacs-gnuseye-jump~ (~/~) is a command palette over
  every entity, named places/countries, and commands (toggle any layer, open
  stats, refresh).
- *Live stats pane* (~S~): per-layer counts, freshness, errors, on-screen
  total, kind tally; debounced off the reindex hook.
- *Bookmarks & tours*: ~m~ saves a viewpoint, ~'~ flies to one, ~O~ starts an
  auto-flying tour; persisted under the config dir.
- *Geofences*: ~cmacs-gnuseye-add-geofence~ / ~-geofence-here~ (~G~) — a
  throttled tick test emits podomation ~on_geofence_enter~ /
  ~on_geofence_exit~ events with the entity data, and can draw a range ring.
- *Time-scrub / replay* (~T~): a bounded ring buffer of position snapshots
  with a header-line scrubber (~<~/~>~ step, ~SPC~ play, ~=~ live).
- *AI intelligence* (cmacs-ai, when built): inspector ~[a]~ asks about the
  selected entity; ~A~ briefs the on-screen region into an Org SITREP; a
  ~hotspots~ layer surfaces co-located multi-kind signal clusters; ~C~
  computes the country-instability index and paints it as a choropleth.
- *Follow + click-through*: ~.~ locks the camera onto the selected entity;
  inspector ~[w]~ opens a marker's live stream (cmacs-video), ~[n]~ its
  article (gsurf).
- *Non-geospatial dashboards*: table feeds (markets, macro) render as
  side-buffer panels via ~cmacs-gnuseye-define-panel~; ~D~ opens the keyless
  market panels (crypto, Fear & Greed, FX, predictions).

* v3: the solar system

The ~Solar system~ category puts the sky on the globe: the Sun, the Moon, the
planets, deep-space probes (Voyager 1/2, New Horizons, JWST), and asteroids,
each rendered at its *true geocentric direction* — directly above its
sub-point (latitude = declination, longitude = RA − GMST), sweeping westward
live as the Earth rotates — on a *log-compressed celestial shell*:

*Distances are linearly true* at 290 world units per AU — one uniform scale,
so the whole orbital geometry is exact: the Sun sits 290 units from Earth,
Jupiter ~5.2× as far from the Sun as Earth is, Neptune at ~29.9 AU.  Two
documented exceptions: the Moon is floored at 26 units (its true scaled
distance, 0.75, would sit inside the Earth globe) and deep-space probes clamp
at the 9000-unit chart edge (Voyager's true 49,000 would sit beyond the
render horizon).

*Sizes follow one √-law for every body, the Sun included* (no arbitrary
caps): rendered radius = 6.371·√(R/R⊕).  The Sun renders 10.4× Earth and
3.2× Jupiter — unmistakably dominant; Jupiter 3.3× Earth; Venus ≈ Earth; the
Moon ≈ half.  Size *order* and the dominance hierarchy are exact; the
inspector notes each body's true ratio, real distance (km / AU), and
light-time.

- ~solar-system~ — Sun + Moon + 7 planets from *local ephemerides* (no
  network, always current): the JPL/Standish approximate planetary elements
  (1800–2050, arcminute class) and the Astronomical Almanac low-precision
  Moon (~0.3°), in ~cmacs-gnuseye-ephem.el~.  Re-computed every smooth tick,
  so the sky moves in real time.  Validated against JPL Horizons fixtures
  in the ERT suite.
- ~probes~ / ~asteroids~ — positions from the keyless *JPL Horizons* API
  (geocentric observer ephemerides), refreshed every 6 h.  Configure the
  body lists with ~cmacs-gnuseye-probes~ and ~cmacs-gnuseye-asteroids~.

Click a body to fly out and frame it (the camera moves just outside its
shell, with Earth behind it); ~.~ tracks it as it moves.  *~0~ (or Home) is
the "go back to Earth" button*: it clears the selection and returns to the
standard Earth orbit view from anywhere in the system.

* Architecture

#+begin_example
 Elisp layers ──set-entities──▶ C core ──render API──▶ globe (libregnum)
 (fetch feeds)  (entity plists)   (markers+payload)     (sphere+markers)
        ▲                                    │
        └──────── click → pick → entity ◀────┘
#+end_example

- *C core* (~cmacs/gnuseye/~) owns the fast/shared/unsafe parts: globe and
  marker rendering, geodesy, satellite propagation, async HTTP, and the
  ray-pick → deferred-dispatch click path.
- *Elisp* owns everything per-source: endpoints, JSON shaping, refresh
  cadence, detail rendering, and toggling.

** Translation-unit firewall

raylib's ~Color~ struct clashes with cmacs's ~Color~ typedef, so only
~cmacs-gnuseye-globe.c~ (the "render half") includes ~<libregnum.h>~; every
other gnuseye source includes ~lisp.h~ and talks to the render half through
the plain-C API in ~cmacs-gnuseye-globe.h~.  Pure geodesy lives in
~cmacs-gnuseye-geomath.h~ (no Lisp, no libregnum) so both halves share it.

This mirrors the libregnum ~scene-*.c~ builder pattern.

* The entity schema

A layer's fetch function returns a list of entity plists.  Only ~:id~,
~:lat~, and ~:lon~ are required:

| Key          | Meaning                                                    |
|--------------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| ~:id~        | stable per-entity key (drives diff/pick).  Required.       |
| ~:lat :lon~  | degrees, WGS84.  Required.                                  |
| ~:alt~       | metres above the sphere (default 0).                       |
| ~:heading~   | degrees clockwise from true north; nil = unoriented.       |
| ~:speed~     | m/s (surfaced in the detail view).                         |
| ~:kind~      | symbol → icon/colour style (satellite/aircraft/ship/…).    |
| ~:label~     | short on-globe label (callsign, name).                     |
| ~:color~     | "#rrggbb" override; nil = derive from ~:kind~.             |
| ~:scale~     | marker size multiplier (default 1.0).                      |
| ~:trail~     | list of (LAT LON ALT) → polyline (orbit/track/wake).       |
| ~:polygon~   | list of (LAT LON) → filled translucent region draped on the globe (alert zone, AOI). |
| ~:label-mode~ | 0 never / 1 selected / 2 hover (default) / 3 always.      |
| ~:detail~    | (lambda (ENTITY) …) override of the layer detail fn.       |
| ~:data~      | opaque record surfaced in the detail view (the escape hatch). |
| ~:ts~        | epoch seconds of this fix.                                  |

The C core normalises ~:kind~ to an integer code and ~:color~ to packed
RGBA, then stores the whole plist as the marker's pick payload — so a click
returns the full record to the layer's detail function.

* Defining a layer

#+begin_src elisp
(cmacs-gnuseye-define-layer openaq
  :title "Air quality (OpenAQ)"
  :group 'weather
  :kind 'storm
  :interval 600
  :default-on nil
  :fetch
  (lambda (cb)
    (cmacs-gnuseye-fetch-json
     "https://api.openaq.org/v3/latest?limit=200"
     (lambda (data)
       (funcall cb
         (mapcar (lambda (r)
                   (list :id (alist-get 'location r)
                         :lat (alist-get 'latitude r)
                         :lon (alist-get 'longitude r)
                         :label (alist-get 'location r)
                         :data r))
                 (alist-get 'results data)))))))
#+end_src

Re-evaluate the form to redefine the layer; toggle it on in the layers
panel.  No rebuild required.

~cmacs-gnuseye-define-layer~ keys: ~:title :group :kind :fetch :interval
:default-on :detail :needs-key :advance :cluster~ (see *Layer lifecycle and
motion* for ~:advance~ and *Globe visualization* for ~:cluster~).

Helpers:
- ~cmacs-gnuseye-fetch-json~ / ~cmacs-gnuseye-fetch-text~ — async GET that
  prefers the native libsoup client and falls back to ~url-retrieve~.  A
  HEADERS alist is supported (e.g. a ~User-Agent~ for NWS).
- ~cmacs-gnuseye-secret~ — resolve an API key from an env var,
  ~$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/cmacs/gnuseye/keys.el~, or a default.
- Geodesy DEFUNs: ~cmacs-gnuseye-latlon-to-xyz~, ~-haversine~, ~-bearing~,
  ~-great-circle~.
- Motion + geometry (Elisp): ~cmacs-gnuseye-dead-reckon~,
  ~-dead-reckon-layer~ (a ready-made ~:advance~), ~-destination~,
  ~-circle-points~ (range rings).
- Globe drawing DEFUNs: ~cmacs-gnuseye-add-polygon~ / ~-clear-polygons~
  (filled regions), ~-add-coastline~ (polylines), ~-add-label~, ~-add-flag~,
  ~-set-sun-time~ (terminator), ~-fly-to~, ~-view-center~.
- Choropleth: ~cmacs-gnuseye-choropleth~ / ~-choropleth-clear~.
- Satellites: ~cmacs-gnuseye-tle-parse~, ~-sat-propagate~, ~-sat-track~.

Everything is *off by default*; enable from the toggle pane.  ~natural-events~
is one rich keyless feed (volcanoes, severe storms, wildfires, icebergs,
floods, landslides).  ~nws-alerts~ draws each US warning as a filled,
severity-coloured zone polygon plus a centroid marker.  ~aurora~ plots the
OVATION oval as a band of probability-coloured dots.  ~gdelt~ geolocates news
for ~cmacs-gnuseye-gdelt-query~, points sized by mention volume.

- *Satellites* fetch TLEs from CelesTrak at most every few hours
  (~cmacs-gnuseye-astro-group~, default "stations") and re-propagate them
  locally to "now" — live motion needs no network.  Between fetches the
  shared smooth-motion tick (~cmacs-gnuseye-smooth~) re-propagates each
  satellite with SGP4 every ~0.4 s so it glides along its orbit (the same
  mechanism that dead-reckons aircraft).  Each satellite carries its
  one-orbit ground track as a trail.
- *Aircraft* come from adsb.lol --- a free, keyless community ADS-B
  aggregator.  By default (~cmacs-gnuseye-air-global~) the whole globe is
  swept with several large overlapping queries
  (~cmacs-gnuseye-air-global-tiles~) and merged by ICAO hex, so *every*
  aircraft adsb.lol can see (typically 5--6 k) is indexed and searchable in
  the entity list.  The tiles all run at once on their own C worker
  threads, a small fast query around the current view fires first, and the
  map fills in progressively as each returns (so planes appear quickly
  rather than after the slowest tile).  Drawing is capped to the nearest
  ~cmacs-gnuseye-render-max~ markers in the current view (default 700; the
  rest stay searchable), re-picked as you navigate, and each plane icon is
  small (~cmacs-gnuseye-air-marker-scale~) and zoom-scaled.  Set
  ~cmacs-gnuseye-air-global~ nil to instead query only the current view
  (lighter), or ~cmacs-gnuseye-air-source~ to ~opensky~ for OpenSky (its
  anonymous API now returns HTTP 429; ~OPENSKY_USER~/~OPENSKY_PASS~ help).
- *Vessels* fetch live AIS from ~cmacs-gnuseye-marine-url~ — by default the
  keyless Digitraffic open AIS (Baltic / Gulf of Finland coverage; fly to
  ~59N 21E~ to see it).  Free *global* AIS is websocket + key; point the URL
  at another regional open-AIS endpoint or a LAN AIS-catcher/rtl-ais server
  (GeoJSON or flat-array shapes both parse).
- *Weather/geo-events* ship earthquakes (on), rocket launches, and NASA
  FIRMS fires (needs ~FIRMS_MAP_KEY~).

* API keys

Resolved by ~cmacs-gnuseye-secret~ in order: environment variable →
~$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/cmacs/gnuseye/keys.el~ (an alist, keep out of version
control) → default.  Relevant names: ~OPENSKY_USER~, ~OPENSKY_PASS~,
~FIRMS_MAP_KEY~.  A layer that declares ~:needs-key~ is shown as disabled
until the key is present.

* libregnum additions

gnuseye needed three small, generic (gnuseye-agnostic) additions to
libregnum:

- A *persistent background-model slot* on the render context, drawn first
  every frame and not cleared by ~clear_drawables~ — so the Earth sphere and
  its textures survive the per-tick marker rebuilds.
- A *per-node label mode* generalising the overlay's label filter
  (~never~/~selected~/~hover~/~always~), so the globe can label
  hovered/selected markers without flooding the screen.
- A single *click router* (~cmacs-libregnum--node-clicked~) so each scene
  mode decides what a node click means; the globe routes it to the entity
  detail view, the tree keeps drilling/visiting files.

* Coordinate system

Right-handed, Y-up, matching libregnum scenes.  The globe is a sphere of
radius 6.371 world units, with 1 unit == 1000 km, so altitudes lift markers
off the surface at a natural scale.  ~lat=0,lon=0~ is at ~(+R,0,0)~; the
north pole at ~(0,+R,0)~; longitude increases eastward toward ~-Z~ (so,
viewed from outside with north up, east is to the right — a standard,
un-mirrored globe).  All vector geography, polygons, rings, and the
day/night sun vector are computed in this same convention so they always
align with the markers.

* Satellite propagation accuracy

v1 uses an exact *two-body (Keplerian)* propagator derived from the TLE mean
elements.  It omits the SGP4 secular/periodic perturbations (J2 nodal
regression, atmospheric drag); over the short windows the globe propagates
(minutes ahead, with TLEs refreshed every few hours) the visual error is
small.  Full SGP4/SDP4 is a future upgrade — vendor a public-domain
reference (Vallado/CelesTrak) and validate against its test vectors.

* Performance notes

Markers and arcs are individual drawables with per-marker pick, capped
around a few thousand at 60 FPS through the FBO.  Layers bound their marker
count (e.g. ~cmacs-gnuseye-air-max~) and should scope fetches to the area of
interest.  A batched dynamic mesh (one draw call, no per-marker pick) is the
escalation path for tens of thousands of contacts.
