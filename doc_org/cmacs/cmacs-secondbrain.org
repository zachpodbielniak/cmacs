* Overview

=M-x cmacs-secondbrain= draws your agentic workspace as four concentric rings around a centre, as a live libregnum scene inside a cmacs buffer. =M-x cmacs-secondbrain-3d= gives the same graph with a free camera.

It is a sibling of [[file:cmacs-roamgraph.org][roamgraph]], not a mode of it. Either can be built and used without the other; what they share is [[file:graphcore.org][cmacs/graphcore]], so layouts, animation and collapse arrive in both.

Requires =--with-cmacs-secondbrain= (part of the standard flag set), which needs =--with-cmacs-libregnum= and =--with-cmacs-glib=.

* The ARMS framework

Four layers, innermost to outermost:

| Ring | What it holds | The question it answers |
|------+---------------+-------------------------|
| *Skills* | what the agent can do | what capabilities exist, and what do they connect to |
| *Memory* | what has accumulated | what context has been built up, and how is it organised |
| *Routines* | what runs unattended | what is automated, and what have I forgotten I automated |
| *Applications* | what it is wired into | what can it reach, and therefore what does it have my trust for |

The ordering is deliberate. Skills sit at the centre because they are what the agent actually /does/; Applications sit at the edge because they are the boundary with everything else. Memory is normally the largest by an order of magnitude.

** Why this is not decoration

roamgraph answers "how are my notes linked?". This answers "what does my system consist of?", and the difference matters because the second question has consequences:

- An application in the outer ring that you no longer use is *standing trust you have not revoked*. If your agent can reach a CRM, it can mail your list.
- A routine you do not recognise is *unattended automation*. It has been running.
- A skill you cannot see is one you will rewrite.

A file browser shows you the same bytes and answers none of that.

* Getting started

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(cmacs-secondbrain)          ; M-x, flat
(cmacs-secondbrain-3d)       ; free camera
#+end_src

The map opens to the rings and their department hubs, each labelled with what it holds. Click a hub — or =TAB= on it — to open one; =e= opens every department at once and =c= shuts them again. Set =cmacs-secondbrain-start-collapsed= to =nil= to open the whole map on load instead.

Once open, a department's members occupy a contiguous *wedge* of the ring sized by how many there are, so a department of four thousand notes gets a proportionally bigger arc than one of four and the two stay legible side by side.

*Hover to interrogate.* Moving the pointer over any node lights up that node and the nodes it links to, and dims the rest — no click, no state change, no way to get lost. Its /links/, not its department: a department is a wedge you can already see, and since every flagged node draws its label, lighting a department of fourteen hundred put fourteen hundred labels over the map. It is suppressed while a search is active, because a search is a deliberate question and a stray pointer movement must not silently answer a different one. =H= toggles it.

** Keys

Press =?= in the map for all of this as a menu that also shows each toggle's current state; =M-?= is the plain-text version with the legend.

*** Moving around

Two positions. The *cursor* — a white ring, always labelled — is what the keys move. The *anchor* — the selection, with its halo and lit links — is what they orbit. Unanchored, =h= =j= =k= =l= roam to the nearest node in that direction; with a node anchored (=SPC=, or a click) the same four keys walk only *that node's links*, still by screen direction, so "right" means "the connection that way". =<escape>= drops the anchor and leaves the cursor where it is, so you can walk out along a node's links, let go, and carry on roaming from there; =SPC= anchors again on whatever the cursor is standing on. =RET= opens the cursor's node.

*** Why single keys work here at all

These viewports sit in Evil *emacs state*, set per major mode. In normal or motion state =h=, =j=, =k=, =l=, =n=, =a=, =e= and =c= are Evil motions and operators, so every one of them would do something else — famously =j= → =evil-next-line= → "end of buffer". =<escape>= leaves for normal state and =C-z= comes back; =C-w= keeps Evil's window commands throughout.

This has to be declared by each mode: `evil-set-initial-state` is looked up per mode, and inheriting from a parent that declares it is not enough. Until it was, the second brain was a mouse-only tool under Doom — every key silently did something else.

** Mouse

| Gesture | Effect |
|---------+--------|
| left click on a department | select it *and expand or fold it* |
| left click on a leaf | select it; the inspector follows |
| double click a node | select it and fly the camera to it |
| left drag a node | move it, and pin it where you drop it |
| (a short drag is still a click) | the drag starts only past a few pixels |
| left drag | orbit (3D), continuously through the poles; no effect in the locked 2D view |
| right drag | pan |
| wheel | zoom, wheel-up towards the scene |
| right click | the context menu for that kind, AI section last |

Clicking a department expands it because that is the whole interaction: the rings tell you what exists, and a click tells you what is inside. Making expand a separate keystroke would mean the obvious gesture on the obvious target does nothing.

*A click does not move the camera.* libregnum's input layer flies to whatever a left click hits — right for a scene you navigate /by/ clicking, wrong here, where the click starts an animation and the camera arriving on top of the hub hides the one thing you clicked to see. Flying is =f=, deliberately, and it keeps =cmacs-secondbrain-fly-context= (0.4 by default) of the whole graph's extent between camera and node: the node's own size says nothing about the scale of the graph around it, so without that floor a fly-to frames one sphere and none of its surroundings.

** Background

=b= picks what fills the viewport behind the graph: =nebula= (the default — layered value noise plus a star field over a vertical gradient), =starfield=, =gradient=, =solid=, =none=, or =image=, which draws a wallpaper file cover-fit so any aspect ratio fills the viewport without being squashed.

Picking =image= offers whatever it finds under =cmacs-secondbrain-wallpaper-dirs= and still accepts any path you type. An unreadable path is refused and reported rather than blanking the viewport.

The procedural kinds are generated once into a texture and cached until the kind, colours or viewport size change, so they cost one blit per frame. They are deterministic — the same viewport gives the same sky every session, which is both less distracting and what makes them testable.

*Screensavers* are a background too, though not the default: set =cmacs-secondbrain-background= to =screensaver= and pick a config with =cmacs-secondbrain-screensaver= (=helios-blue= if you do not). They render in the same out-of-process renderer the animated wallpaper uses, so none of the screensaver's GL runs on Emacs's thread, and the sink they use needs no gowl — this works in a plain pgtk Emacs.

Pick a dark one. The graph is drawn over the top, and a screensaver that fills the frame with bright detail leaves the nodes nowhere to be read against.

If the screensaver subsystem is not built, or the module will not load, the background falls back to =nebula= and says so rather than leaving a blank viewport.

** Labels

Label size is not a fixed pixel count. It is a base
(=cmacs-secondbrain-label-size=, 15) that applies at a viewport
=cmacs-secondbrain-label-reference-height= tall and scales with the
actual viewport from there, up to =cmacs-secondbrain-label-scale-max=.

That indirection earns its keep because the framebuffer now tracks the
window exactly: one number cannot read well in both a half-screen window
and a maximised one on a large display, and a value chosen for the
smaller is unreadably small in the larger. It is re-applied on every
refit, which is precisely when the viewport changed.

The font is baked at 48 px and drawn smaller — downscaling through the
bilinear filter is what keeps the glyphs sharp, and a label drawn
*above* its baked size would be upscaled, which is the blur this avoids.
The plate behind each label is opaque: it exists for the case where the
scene behind the text is a ring of hundreds of nodes with a thousand
links over it, and any translucency there puts that texture straight
through the glyphs.

** Connections

Selecting a note *lights its links and dims everything else*. That is the part that makes them legible: at rest every link is drawn the same, which is honest and useless once there are a few hundred — the answer to "what is this note connected to" is invisible inside its own hairball.

The light travels as a *rope light*: each lit link carries a run of evenly spaced bright beads that all advance together, so the direction is read off the run rather than off any single bead. The link itself is the *tube* — drawn in the node's own colour at moderate brightness — and the beads are nearly white, because lamps only slightly brighter than their tube are not lamps.

That colouring is the other half of making links legible: a lit link says /whose/ link it is, where a grey line says nothing.

It is a fixed pool of beads repositioned every frame, not one drawable per link — a note with a hundred links would otherwise add a hundred drawables on every selection change, and removing them again means a rebuild. The pool is shared out over the lit links, so many links get fewer beads each rather than most links getting none.

=cmacs-secondbrain-link-pulse= turns it off; =-link-pulse-speed= sets how fast. Note the pattern repeats every bead-span: identical, evenly spaced beads advancing by exactly one span give a pixel-identical frame.

*The linked notes show their names.* A lit rope running off to an unlabelled dot says something is connected without saying what. Selecting a note flags everything it links to, and a flagged node gets a label priority tier of its own — eligibility alone is not enough, because labels are capped and the cap ranks by node size, so a small linked note loses to every hub in the scene.

*Selecting a department lights what the department connects to.* A hub carries no org-roam links of its own — only its members do — so matching the selected node alone lit nothing at all. Membership walks the parent chain, so a note nested below a folder below a hub still counts.

Selecting from Lisp — keyboard navigation, search, the inspector — tells the scene too, so those get the halo and the lit links exactly as a click does.

** Dragging and rotation

Left-drag a node to move it. It is *pinned* where you drop it, so the layout leaves it alone; =u= unpins the selection and =U= unpins everything. Empty space still orbits and pans, so the camera controls are unchanged.

=R= turns on a slow rotation. It is the layout's own spin, so the nodes really move and picking follows them — not a camera trick, which would look the same and leave every pick box behind. It costs a layout pass per tick, which is why it is off unless you ask.

** Node shading

raylib draws an unlit sphere in a single colour, and the renderer's real lighting reaches only =MESH_ASSET= models inside the editor build — so a graph of a few thousand spheres has no shading available to it at all, and every node is a flat disc with size as its only depth cue.

Round nodes are therefore drawn by the renderer's *orb pass*: real sphere geometry, shaded per vertex, with correct silhouettes, correct occlusion and correct depth.

*This replaced an impostor*, and the reason is worth writing down because the impostor was the obvious answer and looked right in every screenshot. An impostor is a camera-facing quad carrying a picture of a lit sphere — cheap, round from every angle, and with its highlight baked into *screen space*. Orbiting therefore never changes how anything is lit, nodes never occlude each other properly, and a field of them reads as stickers on a sheet rather than as objects in a space. That is exactly the complaint the 3D view drew.

The lighting is a world-fixed *key* plus a camera-following *fill*, and having both is the point:

- A pure headlight looks like the answer and is the trap. A sphere lit from wherever you happen to be standing is /identical/ from every angle — so the geometry would be real and the picture would still read as a sticker. Real geometry with a headlight buys nothing you can see.
- A pure key light is the opposite trap. Half the graph turns to silhouette as you orbit, and in a map whose colours /are/ the taxonomy an unidentifiable node is a lost node.

Key for form, fill for legibility, plus a rim term so a small node's unlit limb does not fade into the sky, and a specular taken off the key alone so there is one highlight that visibly travels as the scene turns.

*Why this is affordable.* Every orb is the same unit sphere translated and scaled, so every orb's vertex normals are identical and the lighting depends only on the normal and the camera. The shading is therefore computed *once per frame per level of detail* and thousands of orbs cost a multiply per vertex rather than a lighting equation per vertex. On top of that the tessellation is chosen per node: a department hub is a landmark worth triangles, a leaf a few pixels across is not, and past ~1200 visible nodes the leaves drop another tier because at that density you are reading structure, not looking at one ball. Measured at 1200×900 on a 1400-node map: 12.1 ms a frame at the middle tier, 7.0 ms at the low one.

Two details that are load-bearing rather than cosmetic:

- *Winding.* Backface culling is on, and a reversed winding shows the /inside/ of every sphere: still round, lit from the wrong side, and not obviously a bug. The orb mesh is wound exactly as raylib winds =DrawSphereEx=.
- *Orbs are not cleared by =clear-drawables=* — the same trap billboards fall into. The scene clears them itself on rebuild, or every refresh leaves another copy behind at the position each node used to be in, which a screenshot hides because the stale ones sit under the live ones.

Skills and applications keep their own geometry: a gem must show facets and a prism must show edges, and a sphere shows neither. Those get a small offset highlight instead, which is adequate because there are tens of them rather than thousands.

=cmacs-secondbrain-node-shading= turns the whole thing off, and round nodes fall back to raylib's flat unlit spheres.

** Glow

Every node also carries a soft additive halo in its own colour, drawn by a dedicated renderer pass after everything else: additive blending, depth writes off, depth testing on. Each of those three is load-bearing. /Additive/ means a halo can only brighten what is under it, overlapping halos sum instead of occluding, and draw order stops mattering — which is why the pass needs no sorting. /Depth writes off/ is the particle lesson: a translucent quad that writes depth punches an invisible hole nothing behind it can draw through. /Depth testing on/ is what anchors a halo to its node — a glow behind real geometry is hidden, so it reads as light bleeding off the node rather than as a sprite pasted over the scene.

The halo is what seats the map in a dark background: against the nebula a flat-lit glyph reads as a sticker, a glowing one as a light source. It also carries state, in intensity rather than in colour — hubs and the centre glow wider and brighter (they are the wayfinding), search matches glow gold, dimmed nodes barely at all, and the /selection breathes/: its halo swells and settles on the same clock as the travelling link light, which is what makes "this one" unmistakable in a field of steady lights.

The same change fixed a latent artefact in the billboard pass itself: alpha-blended camera-facing quads were drawn in insertion order, and a quad's translucent texels still write depth — so once the camera orbited and two nodes overlapped, the nearer quad's soft edge clipped the farther one into a rectangle. The normal billboard layer is now depth-sorted back-to-front every frame.

=cmacs-secondbrain-node-glow= (default =t=) and the =G= key control it. Like shading, it is decided when the scene is built, so the toggle re-reads the sources.

** Particles

Ambient particles drift from every department hub, coloured by its PARA bucket and breathing wider and faster the more it holds, so the motion carries the same information the size does. Selecting a node and expanding a department each fire a burst — the expand's is bigger and bluer, because it moves the whole department and the burst marks where from.

They are decoration and nothing about the map's meaning depends on them. They are on because a system you are meant to feel ownership of should look alive; =P= turns them off, and =cmacs-secondbrain-particles= is the defcustom.

*The animation clock stops when nobody is looking.* A tick renders the whole scene synchronously, and Emacs runs timers whenever it /waits/ — including inside =accept-process-output= during startup. A clock left running for a buffer no window is showing therefore renders on top of whatever Emacs is trying to do, indefinitely. Since the link pulse has no end state, a selected node used to keep that clock alive forever; a session hung at startup doing exactly this. The timer now disarms itself the first time it finds the buffer unseen, and =window-configuration-change-hook= starts it again when the buffer comes back on screen.

The honest cost: particles advance on wall-clock time inside the render pass, so the view keeps a redraw clock up at =cmacs-secondbrain-particle-fps= (24) even when nothing is happening. Turning particles off stops the clock with them.

*** The framebuffer must match its window

=cmacs-secondbrain--fit-window-now= resizes the framebuffer to the window's *body* pixel size — the text area, with fringes, margins and the mode line excluded — and =window-size-change-functions= re-runs it, coalesced through a 0.06 s idle timer, whenever the window changes.

This is not cosmetic, and neither is the word /body/. The picture, the click mapping and the framebuffer are a three-party contract: the pgtk overlay and the =--lrg= backend paint the view into =window_box TEXT_AREA=, the input layer maps clicks against that same box, and this hook sizes the FBO to match. Any party on a different rect puts every click somewhere other than where it was aimed. Both failure modes have shipped: before the hook existed nothing was clickable at all, and later the /paint/ used the full window rect against a text-area click mapping — offset by the fringes and the mode line's share of the height — so every node had to be clicked above and to the right of where it was drawn.

* Filtering by tag and category

"Show me only the notes about X." The map's other narrowing tools are structural — a ring, a department, the selection's neighbourhood. Tags and categories are the one axis that cuts /across/ the structure: a =finance= tag lives in projects, areas and resources at once, and it is the axis the author chose, note by note, so it is the most deliberate signal the notes carry.

Memory nodes carry their org =#+filetags:= (either form, =:a:b:= or =a b=) as =:tags= and their =#+category:= as =:category=, from the org-roam database when there is one (tags from its =tags= table, the category from the node's stored properties) and from the file scanner when there is not.

*** The palette — =F t=

A pane beside the map listing every tag and every category, most used first. Each row is a toggle: a coloured bar for the tag's weight (logarithmic — the counts span three orders of magnitude, and on a linear scale every tag but the biggest is one cell wide), the name, the count. =RET=, =SPC= or a click flips it; =a= switches between keeping nodes with /any/ active tag and with /all/ of them; =c= clears; =n=/=p= (or =j=/=k=) walk the rows; =/= searches the palette itself. The header line says what is active and how many nodes survive. A tag's colour is hashed from its name, so it is the same every session.

*** The quick picker — =F f=

=completing-read-multiple= over the same tags (categories prefixed =@=), each annotated with its count, for when you already know the name. An empty choice clears the filter.

*** What the filter does to the map

The active tags reduce to a *keep set* of ids: tags combine per =cmacs-secondbrain-filter-mode= (=any= by default; =all= on request), categories combine as /any/ among themselves, and when both kinds are active a node must satisfy both — switching on a category on top of a tag can only mean "and within this category". Every ancestor of a kept node is kept too, so the department a survivor lives in stays lit and the filtered map still shows /where/ the matches are; a lone bright dot in a dim ring says less than a lit wedge does.

The set is handed to the scene in one call (=cmacs-secondbrain-set-keep-set=), which dims everything outside it *at paint time* — the same mechanism as the ring filter, deliberately: it composes with the ring filter, a search match still lights through it, and it never fights the search over the DIM flag, which belongs to search alone. An active filter that matches nothing reaches the scene as an *empty vector*, because in Lisp the empty list is =nil= and =nil= means "lift the filter"; the honest picture of "the notes tagged X" when there are none is a dark map, not an unfiltered one. The mode line shows the active filter as =[#finance #fire @money]=; =z= clears it along with everything else.

* The dressing: lanes, a core, a sky, breath, and the fly-in

Everything in this section is decoration. None of it changes what the map means, and =M-d=, =*= and one =setq= each turn it off. It is on because a system you are meant to feel ownership of should look like somewhere, and a graph of lit balls on a dark gradient, however correct, looks like a diagram.

*** Band lanes

Each ARMS band gets three soft additive fills in its own colour: a skirt rising to the hub circle, a fill across the rows the members sit in, and a skirt falling away outside. The peak is on the hub circle, where the crisp guide line runs (drawn a little brighter over a lane than it needed to be alone, because the sharp line is what the eye locks onto in the soft fill). They follow the layout's own band radii — the same rule the guides follow, for the same reason — and the galaxy warp, so they bend with the disc. Widths are fractions of the ring gap and intensities are low on purpose: a lane is a place, not a light, and the nodes have to stay the brightest thing on it. Together they turn four thin hoops into four bands of light the nodes lie /in/, which is what makes the disc read as a galaxy's dust lanes.

*** The galactic core

A disc fading out toward the innermost band, and three glows stacked on the origin: white-hot, then warm, then a cool corona, each wider than the last. Sized from the innermost band so it stays inside the Skills ring however the map has grown — a fixed size would swallow a small map and vanish in a large one. The centre node sits inside all of it and comes out white-hot, which is the right thing to happen to the one node everything else hangs off.

*** A sky that is part of the world

=cmacs-secondbrain-starfield= (1600) stars sit on a shell 420 world units out — outside any map this draws, inside the far clip plane at 1000. This is not the =starfield= *background*, which is a picture and does not move when the camera does. These parallax under orbit, and that parallax is most of what tells the eye the scene has depth. The sky is generated from a fixed seed so it is the same every session, and it twinkles gently on the renderer's effect clock.

*** Breathing halos

=cmacs-secondbrain-glow-breath= (0.35) lets every node's halo drift in brightness on its own period and phase, so no two neighbours pulse in step. Brightness, never hue: the category colours survive it. It needs a running clock to be seen, which the particles keep; turn particles off and the halos hold still along with them.

*** The opening fly-in

Opening the map (=cmacs-secondbrain-intro=, =cmacs-secondbrain-intro-seconds= 1.8) fits the view and then flies the camera in onto it from further out, higher, and swung seventy degrees round — so the disc is seen turning in depth once before you touch it. Ease-out cubic: fast off the mark, gentle landing, and it lands /exactly/ on the fitted pose, because a fly-in that ended a little off would be a small jump on every open, forever. In the flat view the orbit is locked, so the start is a straight pull-back — a zoom in, not a swing. =M-i= replays it. The fly-in rides the transition clock and is a reason for it to keep running; a hard stop (the buffer hidden) drops it, or it would jump the camera back to mid-flight when the buffer next appeared.

*** Why these are counts in the tests, and frames only when they must be

A lane at rest alpha barely moves a frame mean, so the tests count ribbons and glows — four bands × three lanes + one disc = thirteen ribbons, three core glows — and prove the renderer drew them with one frame comparison against an empty scene. The sky is asserted by frame *bytes* across a quarter turn, because a shell is roughly isotropic and its mean barely moves; the breath by pinning the effect clock (=cmacs-libregnum-set-effect-time=) and requiring two instants to differ and one instant to agree.

* The 3D view is a galaxy

Concentric rings viewed in three dimensions are coplanar, so orbiting them only proves they are flat — the third dimension buys nothing. So in 3D the disc is *warped*, the way a galaxy is warped.

*** Two shapes, and why the default is the boring one

=cmacs-secondbrain-galaxy-shape= picks between them; =M-t= toggles.

- *flare* (the default) is a *saucer*. The height depends only on the radius, so the disc dips through the middle and curves up evenly all the way round.
- *warp* is the mode-1 "integral sign" a real galaxy has: one side of the disc lifts and the opposite side drops.

The warp is the astrophysically honest shape and it is not the default, because it has a *direction*. Its node line — where the height crosses zero — is a fixed axis in world space, and when you orbit until you are looking along it, the whole map presents as a diagonal streak across the screen. That does not read as depth; it reads as the picture being crooked. The saucer is rotationally symmetric, so its silhouette is the same from every azimuth and the map reads level however you have turned it.

Both grow with the *square* of the radius rather than linearly, and that exponent is the whole feature. The linear version reads correctly in the source and is a rigid rotation in the picture: with height = =r·tan(t)·sin(a)= and the in-plane coordinate =v = r·sin(a)=, the height is exactly =tan(t)·v= — a *plane*. Every node moves, nothing bends, and orbiting reveals precisely the flat disc the warp exists to get rid of. It shipped that way.

The saucer rises from zero at the centre rather than being shifted down to straddle the plane. Centring it vertically in the layout would put the inner rings a long way below a plane they are only a few units from, and =atan(h/r)= — the angle TILT is defined in — runs away toward 90° as =r= goes to zero. The camera centres the height axis instead, which is the honest place for that correction.

Two terms, and the split is the design:

- The *bend* is whichever of the two shapes is selected.
- The *thickness* is a per-node offset so one department's members are not perfectly coplanar. Without it the map is a bent sheet; with it the disc has substance. Unlike the warp it does /not/ vary with azimuth — a real disc is thick everywhere, not only where it bends — and it is a fraction of the same =r·tan(tilt)= amplitude the warp uses, so the two stay in proportion at any tilt and any scale.

=cmacs-secondbrain-galaxy-tilt= (32°) is the warp's crest: there a node sits exactly that far above the plane. The thickness rides on top, so an individual node can sit beyond it — but by a stated amount, never past =atan(1.34 · tan TILT)=. The number describes the shape of the disc, not a ceiling on any one node. =T= cycles flat → gentle → default → steep.

The height is computed from the node's *final* azimuth, spin included, so the warp is fixed in world space and a spinning ring flows through it the way stars orbit through a real warped disc, rather than the whole sheet rotating rigidly.

Two scope notes. It applies to the *rings* layout only — a hex lattice bent along an azimuth it does not have would just be crooked. And it needs no branch on the view: a 2D layout snaps every node into the plane, so the setting is simply inert in the flat view and =v= can toggle back and forth without anything to restore.

*** The disc lies in the ground plane

In 3D the rings lie in the world's *ground plane* — X and Z, with the galaxy's height on Y. In 2D they lie in XY, because that is what "right and up" mean on a screen.

That is not bookkeeping; it is the difference between a map you can turn and one you cannot. The renderer's world is Y-up and so is its orbit camera, whose two drag gestures are azimuth about Y and elevation from the XZ plane. Laid out in XY instead, neither gesture means anything sensible: the elevation drag walks the camera along the disc's *own* plane until it jams against the pole clamp edge-on, so the galaxy presents as a flat streak and the drag appears to stop for no reason the picture explains. In XZ the same two gestures are "spin the galaxy" and "raise your eye above it".

*** The guides go where the bands actually are

A ring guide is drawn at the radius the *layout* placed that band at, read back through =cmacs_graph_layout_band_radius=. That radius is *not* a function of the ring's index: it grows with the band's population, so a department of a thousand notes gets the circumference to spread over instead of piling onto itself.

Computing it from the index instead — which is what the guides did at first, and which produces a perfectly plausible number — put them at 6/12/18/24 while the bands sat at 6/29/41/45. Four small hoops adrift in the middle of a map five times their size, which is what "why are the rings off to one side?" looks like. =cmacs-secondbrain-band-radius= exposes the real number, and a test asserts the guide passes through the hubs of the ring it names.

Each guide is a *polyline*, not an =LrgCircle3D=, because the band it names is not flat: it samples the layout's own warp so the two stay in step at any tilt. (A flat circle would also need turning a quarter turn about X, since raylib draws a 3D circle in XY — with the polyline the plane is just where the points are put.)

*** Framing is on the origin, not on the bounding box

A closed-form layout is built around the origin *in the plane*, so that is what the camera aims at there. The two are not the same point: a department's members fan outward from wherever its wedge happens to be, and the outermost ring may hold four nodes at four arbitrary angles, so the bounding box is lopsided — and framing it slides the concentric structure the whole map is built around off into a corner of its own cloud.

The *height* axis is the exception, and is centred on the content: the layout has no symmetry there, and a saucer rises from its centre, so aiming at =y = 0= would hang the whole map in the top of the frame.

Only the *force* layout, which puts the graph wherever it settles, is framed by its box on all three axes.

*** The orbit runs all the way round

A galaxy has no north, so the 3D view turns on the renderer's *continuous orbit*: the elevation runs through a full turn rather than stopping about ten degrees short of each pole, and the camera's up vector flips as it crosses one, which is what keeps the picture continuous instead of snapping through a gimbal. Horizontal drag is negated while inverted, so dragging right still turns the scene the way it moves on screen.

The clamp remains the default elsewhere, and is right there: on gnuseye's globe north stays up and the view never goes upside down.

The subtlety worth knowing if you touch it: elevation cannot be recovered from the camera position alone, because =asin= only ever answers in [-90°, 90°]. The camera's up vector carries the other half of the state. Read it back without also correcting the azimuth by half a turn and the camera teleports to the mirror-image viewpoint on the first inverted drag — a valid view, and completely wrong.

The per-node thickness is hashed from the node's *id*, not its index: index order churns on every rebuild, so hashing that would reshuffle every height on refresh and the map would twitch for no reason.

* Layouts

Four, switchable at any time, and every switch *animates* rather than teleporting — a change that moves every node at once gives the eye no way to follow which node went where.

- *Rings* — the ARMS layout, and the default. Four concentric bands, departments as contiguous arcs.
- *Circle* — one concentric circle per department, ignoring rings.
- *Hex* — a hex lattice. Dense and regular; good for seeing how /many/ of something there is.
- *Force* — the same Fruchterman–Reingold solver roamgraph uses, for when you want the structure to speak rather than the taxonomy.

Ring spin (=s= / =S=) rotates the bands. Alternate bands counter-rotate, so the motion reads as depth rather than as the whole figure sliding.

* Navigating from the keyboard

Four tiers, mirroring =cmacs-roamgraph= so the siblings feel like one tool.

- *Spatial* (=h j k l=, arrows) — the nearest node inside a screen-space cone in that direction. What you reach for when you can see where you want to be. Moves are *exactly reversible*: a cone metric is not self-inverse, so going back the way you came is replayed from a trail rather than recomputed, and =l= then =h= returns you rather than landing somewhere new. The cone is wider here than in roamgraph (=cmacs-secondbrain-cone-degrees=, 50°) because a ring layout puts your neighbour at an angle from you rather than beside you.
- *Topological* (=]= =[= =>= =<= =o=) — walk links, and walk the department you are standing in. Links are treated as undirected: the hub→member edges point one way and the org-roam links the other, and someone tracing connections does not care which end the data happened to store first. =]= will not immediately walk back the way you arrived, so it cannot degenerate into a two-node oscillation.
- *Hierarchical* (=^= =m=) — the ARMS tier, which roamgraph has no equivalent of: a node belongs to a department, which belongs to a ring.
- *Camera* (=f= =+= =-= =0=) — =f= flies to the selection /and re-aims the camera's target at it/, so zooming and orbiting afterwards revolve around that node instead of around wherever the view happened to be centred. =+= and =-= then close and open that distance; each notch takes a fixed fraction of what is left, so steps shrink as you approach and you cannot overshoot.
- *Search* (=/= =n= =N= =J= =o=) — below.

** The camera follows only when it must

A spatial step deliberately does *not* move the camera: the node you stepped to is normally right there, and moving the view on every keystroke makes the whole map swim under you.

But the ring layout is much larger than the viewport, so a few steps in one direction walk the selection off the edge — and from then on you are navigating something you cannot see, which is worse than either extreme. So the camera follows on exactly the condition that matters: when the new selection is *not on screen*, inset by =cmacs-secondbrain-edge-margin= (48 px) so a node half-clipped by the edge counts as off-screen rather than visible. =cmacs-secondbrain-keep-selection-visible= turns it off.

** Navigation reveals

The map opens collapsed, so most of what you can search for is not on screen — and selecting a node with no drawable sets no scene selection, draws no halo and moves no camera. That is indistinguishable from the key having done nothing.

So every keyboard jump goes through one choke point that *expands the ancestors it needs first*, outermost first, then flies the camera. It is a single function precisely so that no individual command can forget.

* Search

Three tiers, cheapest first. The ordering is the point: most of a lookup needs no intelligence at all, so spend none.
Search never re-lays-out the map: it recolours in place, so the shape you were reading stays the shape you are reading. =/= matches as you type — see the hits appear and disappear across the rings, which is the thing a completion list cannot tell you and the entire reason to have a visualiser. RET keeps the highlight so =n= and =N= stay live in the viewport; =C-g= clears it and restores the selection you started from.

*A match inside a collapsed department lights the department.* Without that the feature is dead on the map people actually see: it opens fully collapsed, so every hit resolves to a node with no drawable and the search highlights an empty screen. Expanding everything instead is not the answer either — two thousand hits would bury the answer in the noise the search exists to cut through. So the departments holding your matches light up, and =n= walks into them one at a time, expanding as it goes.


1. *Substring* (=/=) — instant, over the loaded node set. Most of what you look for you already know the name of.
2. *Semantic* (=?=) — embeds the query once and ranks against the brigade notes index. Reach for it when you do not know the name.
3. *Similarity edges* (=M-x cmacs-secondbrain-add-similarity-edges=) — materialises "these are about the same thing" as edges you can see.

Matching nodes take an accent colour and everything else dims, in place — recolouring on every keystroke must not mean rebuilding the scene.

** Similarity edges, in roamgraph too

=M-x cmacs-roamgraph-similarity-edges= does the same for the knowledge graph. roamgraph has carried the =sim= edge kind since before anything emitted one: parsed, weighted 0.35 in the solver so it nudges without competing with real structure, rendered violet so it is never mistaken for a link you wrote, and excluded from =[= / =]= navigation.

* Focusing the view

Three ways to make the map answer one question at a time, all composable with search and with each other:

- *Isolate* (=x=) — while a node is selected, only it, its subtree and its direct neighbours keep their colour; the rest recedes to a third and its links go near-invisible. Selecting a /department/ isolates the department and everything it reaches — which is "show me what this connects to" as one keystroke. The mode follows the selection as it moves, and with nothing selected it is armed but inert.
- *Ring filter* (=F=) — cycles all → Applications → Routines → Memory → Skills → all. Everything outside the kept ring dims. The centre stays lit: it belongs to every ring, and a filtered map with its own centre dimmed looks broken rather than filtered.
- *Age fade* (=a=) — recolours Memory by modification time: a note touched today keeps its full PARA colour, one untouched for =cmacs-secondbrain-age-fade-days= (180) fades most of the way to a cold grey — toward grey, not black, because against a dark background black is /gone/ and stale should read as /faded/. Department hubs wear their members' *mean* age, which matters because the default map is fully collapsed: a fade that only touched leaves would render the default view pixel-identical with the feature on and off. A whole department gone grey is a department you have stopped tending.

Two design rules keep these honest. First, isolate and the ring filter are /paint-time decisions/, not flag writes: the DIM flag belongs to the search machinery, and a second writer would have to agree with it about when to clear — the classic two-owners bug. Second, a search MATCH lights up through both — you searched for it, so a filter never hides it.

The mode line shows =[ring]=, =[isolate]= and =[age]= while any of them is active, so a filtered map cannot be mistaken for the whole map.

* AI actions

Right-click any node and the shared AI menu is there, plus four questions this view makes askable:

- *Explain this skill / routine / application* — what it is and what it is for.
- *What would break without this?* — the trust question the Applications ring exists for. An honest "probably nothing" is the most useful answer it can give, because that is what you were looking for.
- *Is this stale?* — superseded, abandoned, or referring to things that no longer exist.
- *Summarise this department* — offered only on a hub.

All of them answer through =cmacs-ai-textops-stream=, so the reply lands in the output window with =C-c C-c= to follow up and =g= to retry.

Because the view registers a target resolver, =cmacs-ai-view-context= sees it too: a chat turn knows which node you are looking at without being told.

* Panes

=i= opens the inspector on the right: name, role, ring, department, path, the actions available and the node's connections in both directions. It refreshes as you select, because an inspector showing the previous node is worse than no inspector — it is confidently wrong about what you just clicked.

=p= opens a preview on the left, rendering the node's file inline with its own major mode's fontification. This is what makes a skill readable without leaving the map — the thing a graph view usually cannot do. =W= closes both.

Other packages can add inspector actions:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(cmacs-secondbrain-register-inspector-action
 "R" "run it" (lambda (id) ...))
#+end_src

The binding is installed for Evil's normal and motion states too — without that half a registered =a= would just be =evil-append= under Doom, and the action would look registered while doing nothing.

* Application icons

Applications-ring nodes draw their real SVG logo when one is available, rasterised through libregnum's =LrgVectorImage= and uploaded as a texture, so it stays crisp at any zoom rather than being a scaled bitmap.

*No icons ship with cmacs* — application logos are other people's trademarks. =cmacs-secondbrain-icon-dirs= lists where to look (=~/.claude/icons= and the system hicolor scalable theme by default); names are matched case- and separator-insensitively, so "Google Drive" finds =google-drive.svg=. A node without an icon simply keeps its glyph, and an SVG that will not load returns nil rather than failing the build.

=cmacs-secondbrain-show-icons= turns the whole thing off.

* Where the data comes from

Everything is a registered *source*. Adding a ring member is a registration, not a patch:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(cmacs-secondbrain-register-source
 :name 'my-thing :ring 'applications :label "My thing"
 :enumerate (lambda () (list (list :id "x" :title "X" :kind 'app))))
#+end_src

Shipped sources cover two worlds, because both are real:

| Ring | Claude Code workspace | cmacs-native |
|------+-----------------------+--------------|
| Applications | MCP servers from =~/.claude.json=, installed plugins | connected subsystems (gsurf, dbexplorer, MCP, voice) |
| Routines | =~/.claude/scheduled-tasks= | brigade schedules, podomation pods |
| Memory | — | the PARA notes tree |
| Skills | =~/.claude/skills/*/SKILL.md=, =~/.claude/agents= | brigade registered tools |

=cmacs-secondbrain-enabled-sources= selects which contribute. A source you do not use is not merely noise: an Applications ring listing things you never connected tells you nothing about your actual trust surface.

*A source that signals is caught, reported and skipped.* One unreadable config file costs you one ring member, not the map.

** Memory is PARA

The Memory ring groups by [[file:cmacs-para.org][PARA]] bucket — =00_inbox=, =01_projects=, =02_areas=, =03_resources=, =04_archives=, with =02_areas/dailies= split out because it is roughly 40% of a mature tree and would otherwise swamp the areas colour. The tree is already organised; grouping it by anything else would be inventing a second organisation on top of the real one.

Notes are read from =org-roam.db= when it exists. When it does not — org-roam builds it on demand, and roamgraph's native C scanner is a stub — a built-in scanner walks the tree for =:ID:= drawers and =[[id:]]= links instead. Without that fallback the largest of the four rings comes up empty on a machine whose notes are perfectly well formed.

* Ingesting: the front door

The visualiser answers /what is in here/. Ingesting is how things get in. =M-x cmacs-secondbrain-ingest= (or =I= in the visualiser, or =emacsctl sb ingest=, or the =secondbrain_ingest= MCP tool) takes a file, a directory, a URL, a recording, a YouTube video, a web page, a mail, a spreadsheet or a paragraph of text and produces *one Org note*: a real org-roam node with an =:ID:=, the repository's standard header keywords, an AI summary, the extracted content, related notes linked by id, and a bullet in the =00_index.org= of the directory it landed in — a directory chosen by the PARA rules, by you, or by the model.

This is the port of =sbi=, the shell script that did the same job for a Neovim-and-Markdown second brain, onto what cmacs already has. Nothing writes Markdown; nothing shells out to another AI wrapper.

** What goes in

| Kind | Read with | Notes |
|------+-----------+-------|
| =.org= | natively | the header is re-read (title, filetags) and rewritten; an existing =:ID:= is kept |
| Markdown | pandoc, else a built-in reader | GFM tables, fences, task boxes |
| reStructuredText, LaTeX, RTF | pandoc | |
| plain text | natively | prose becomes one-line paragraphs; code-shaped text becomes a block |
| HTML, saved pages | libxml + pandoc (shr without pandoc) | the article is found and the chrome dropped; a SingleFile =saved from= URL is kept |
| PDF | pdftotext, else mutool | pages split on form feeds; a scanned PDF says so |
| EPUB | pandoc, else unzip + libxml | spine order |
| MOBI, AZW | calibre (=ebook-convert=, flatpak found) to EPUB | |
| =.docx .xlsx .pptx .odt .ods .odp= | cmacs-office | sheets become Org tables; slides become sections |
| =.doc .xls .ppt= | LibreOffice (flatpak found) to OOXML, then cmacs-office | |
| CSV, TSV, JSON, XML, YAML, TOML | natively | tables up to =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-table-max-rows=, source blocks beyond |
| =.eml=, mbox, Maildir files | mm-decode | headers decoded, text part preferred, attachments listed |
| =.msg= | msgconvert | |
| audio, video | ffmpeg → embedded whisper | timestamped paragraphs |
| YouTube | yt-dlp captions, else audio → whisper | metadata, description and chapters kept |
| any URL | url.el (or an offscreen gsurf view) | by content type: HTML, PDF, JSON, text |
| =--crawl URL= | BFS, same site, robots.txt honoured | one note per page under a site index |
| =.zip=, =.tar= of saved pages | unpacked | as a site |
| stdin, a region, the clipboard | as text | the format is guessed: org, Markdown, JSON, HTML, prose |

A format is handled by an ordered list of *strategies*, tried until one produces a document — cmacs-native readers first, pandoc second, narrower external tools last. When every strategy is unavailable the error names the programs that would have helped, which on an immutable host is the useful answer. =M-x cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-queue= then =!= shows the doctor: what this machine can read.

Two habits of the old script are deliberately not reproduced. It hard-wrapped everything, because pandoc does unless told not to; every conversion here passes =--wrap=none=. And it downloaded YouTube audio and transcribed it even when the video had captions; here captions are asked for first.

** Where it goes

The order of precedence:

1. =:directory= — an exact root-relative directory.
2. =:para= + =:category= — a PARA category and a sub path, e.g. =resources= + =technical/linux=. A category without a sub path goes to its =personal/= subdirectory (=cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-default-scope=). A sub path that does not exist is created with its own =00_index.org=, linked from the parent index, when =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-create-directories= is on.
3. The model — when nothing is given and =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-placement= is =detect= (the default). It is handed the *real* directory tree (dirs only, depth-limited, archives and dailies excluded) and the repository's placement heuristics, and answers with a path, a confidence and a reason. The path must exist under the root, must start with a PARA category, must not be the archive; below =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-placement-min-confidence= it is ignored. Anything the model proposes that fails these checks goes to =00_inbox= with the proposal recorded in the note's metadata, so a wrong guess costs one triage rather than a misfiled note.
4. =00_inbox= otherwise.

A =:para= given without a sub path still lets the model choose *within* that category.

** What the model does, and does not

One analysis call returns JSON: a title, a one-line description, tags, the summary type to use and the placement. One summary call writes the summary under the type's template — the twenty-seven types of the old script (=meeting=, =book=, =lecture=, =youtube=, =legal=, =cleanup=, …), rewritten to produce Org that nests under =* Summary=. =auto= lets the model pick the type; =--principle= adds a principles section; =--prompt= appends your own instructions.

The model does *not* choose related notes. Those come from the brigade memory index (semantic and lexical, fused) restricted to the notes root, and only hits with an =:ID:= are linked — a model asked to name related notes invents plausible ones, and a dangling id is worse than no link.

The default provider is =claude-code= with =sonnet=. The CLI runs in a directory with no project in it, with the per-machine sections of its default system prompt excluded, because a Claude Code started inside a source checkout believes it is a coding agent in that project: the first live run answered a JSON request with a question about the ingest code it found around itself.

When the model is unavailable, or =--no-ai= is given, the note is still written: source title, no summary, no suggested tags, the given place or the inbox.

Transcripts get =* Transcript= with =[M:SS]= paragraph stamps; a YouTube video also gets =* Description= and =* Chapters=; a crawl gets a site directory with a =00_index.org= carrying the summary and one note per page. Paragraphs are one line each, never hard-wrapped.

File names are the title's slug (=cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-filename-style= adds a date prefix). A collision uniquifies by default; =--append= adds a dated section to the existing note instead, which is what =sbi= did.

** Redaction

=--sanitize= redacts before the model sees the material and before it is stored. The rules are the old =sanitize_data= set, ported, but the ones that cannot tell a secret from a number are *off by default*: the old "bank account" rule was =\b\d{8,17}\b=, which redacted every Unix timestamp and every eight-digit ticket id. =--sanitize-rules email,phone,bank-account= names rules; =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-redaction-rules= is the table.

** Driving it

- =M-x cmacs-secondbrain-ingest= — a file, directory or URL, with a PARA prompt. =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-region=, =-buffer=, =-clipboard= (the =clip2brain= port), =-dired= (marked files), =-record= (record, stop, transcribe, ingest — the =record_audio_and_ingest= port).
- =M-x cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-queue= — the queue: =a= add file, =u= add URL, =p= clipboard, =RET= visit the note, =l= log, =k= cancel, =r= retry, =s= toggle summaries, =P= toggle placement, =!= doctor.
- =emacsctl sb ingest [FLAGS] URL|FILE|-= — from a shell; =-w= waits for the notes. =second-brain= and =secondbrain= are aliases of =sb=. See the emacsctl manual.
- =secondbrain_ingest= — the MCP tool; an agent polls =secondbrain_ingest_status=. Also =secondbrain_tree=, =secondbrain_find= and =secondbrain_doctor=.
- =org.cmacs.Editor1.SecondBrain= — the D-Bus interface behind both.
- =(cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-run INPUTS &rest OPTS)= — from Elisp; =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-wait= for scripts.

Every stage that takes time is asynchronous — a subprocess with a sentinel, a fetch callback, a whisper callback, a model stream — because the same code runs when a D-Bus call arrives on the main thread of a cmacs that may be someone's Wayland compositor. The queue is global, not buffer-local, because jobs are addressed by id from outside Emacs.

Two hooks: =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-after-note-functions= receives an INFO plist (=:file :id :title :kind :source :dir :tags :summary :related=) after every note, and =cmacs-secondbrain-ingest-after-job-functions= receives the job at done, failed or cancelled.

** What costs something

Three settings have a standing cost rather than a one-off one, which is
why each is off or conservative by default:

- =cmacs-secondbrain-background= set to =screensaver= runs a second
  process rendering continuously, and holds a redraw clock up for as
  long as the buffer lives. The procedural backgrounds are generated
  once into a texture and then cost one blit per frame.
- =cmacs-secondbrain-particles= holds a redraw clock at
  =-particle-fps= even when nothing is happening. That is the honest
  price of ambient motion; turning particles off stops the clock.
- =cmacs-secondbrain-auto-rotate= costs a layout pass per tick, because
  it turns the layout rather than the camera — which is what makes
  picking follow the nodes.

=cmacs-secondbrain-link-pulse= costs a recolour pass over the edges per
frame, but only while something is selected.

- *The dressing* is thirteen ribbons of 96 stations and three glows — nothing. *The sky* is 1600 quads in one batch, also nothing; set =cmacs-secondbrain-starfield= to 0 if you would rather the parallax were not there. *Breathing* is one sine per halo per frame, and only costs anything at all because it wants the particle clock running.

** Turning the whole thing down

For a still, cheap view:

#+begin_src elisp
(setq cmacs-secondbrain-background 'solid
      cmacs-secondbrain-particles nil
      cmacs-secondbrain-link-pulse nil
      cmacs-secondbrain-auto-rotate 0.0)
#+end_src

With all four off the view redraws only when something changes.

* Reading the map

Shape is the primary signal; the map should be readable before any label resolves.

| Glyph | Means |
|-------+-------|
| hex prism | an application |
| sphere with a wide ring | a collapsed department, sized by what it hides |
| sphere | a note or file |
| faceted gem | a skill |

A collapsed hub's radius grows with the *cube root* of its descendant count: a department hiding 12000 files must read as bigger than one hiding 300 without swallowing the frame. Linear makes the large one absurd; logarithmic flattens the two into the same dot.

Colours are per ring, fixed rather than hashed, so a colour means the same thing in every session. Memory nodes take their PARA bucket's colour instead.

* Architecture

Three translation-unit classes, the roamgraph split:

| File | Includes | Why |
|------+----------+-----|
| =cmacs-secondbrain-scene.c= | =<libregnum.h>= | raylib's =Color= clashes with pgtk's |
| =cmacs-secondbrain-defuns.c= | =lisp.h= | the Elisp boundary |
| =cmacs-secondbrain-model.c= | neither, only glib | so the ring vocabulary is testable headless |
| =cmacs/graphcore/= | neither, only glib | the shared store and solver |

C owns geometry, the rings, the pick ray and the node table. Elisp owns where the data comes from, what a click means and every pane. C knows nothing about MCP servers, cron or org files. The contract is one DEFUN, =cmacs-secondbrain-set-graph=.

*Identity.* libregnum scene node ids are insertion indices and churn on every rebuild, so every piece of state keys on the source-assigned id string — which is what the C side stores as the node's =path= and what arrives synchronously in the click dispatch.

** Not implemented

Honest list:

- *Skills are faceted gems, not four-pointed stars.* The reference design draws a star; that needs a custom mesh.
- *No node dragging.* =dragging_object= is gated on =editor_active= in the libregnum input layer; it needs a new dispatch branch there.
- *No folders view.* Departments group by PARA bucket or by source; grouping by the literal directory tree is not wired.
- *Similarity edges are computed on demand, not maintained.* Re-running after the notes change is manual.
- *No icons ship with cmacs.* Application logos are other people's trademarks. =cmacs-secondbrain-icon-dirs= points at where you may already have them; a node without one keeps its glyph.
- *No OCR.* A scanned PDF ingests as a note that says it has no text layer; run =ocrmypdf= first.
- *Outlook =.msg= needs =msgconvert=.* Without it the job fails with that message.
- *The crawler is a fetcher, not a browser.* Pages that render only in JavaScript need =--web gsurf=, and the crawl itself always uses url.el.
- *The memory index is not updated incrementally.* A new note becomes findable by =sb find= after the next =cmacs-brigade-memory-build=.

* Tests

=test/cmacs/cmacs-secondbrain-tests.el=, 24 tests in three tiers:

- *Registry and collection* — registration, replacement, enabled-source filtering, ring inheritance, hub/centre synthesis, and that a *failing source is contained*.
- *The scanner* — reads =:ID:= and =[[id:]]= from a temporary tree, drops dangling links, ignores non-roam files.
- *The viewport* (needs a display) — attach/detach, collapse round-trip, payload round-trip including unknown keys, layout switching, *rings ordered outward*, tween completion, projection toggle, empty graph, and the menu adapting to node kind.

Plus =just test-graphcore= for the shared engine: 25 more, fully headless.
